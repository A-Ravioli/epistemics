# Evidence: handwriting, drawing and reading ink

The evidence behind [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), compiled 2026-10-09 from web searches. Each entry has a
confidence rating. Items marked **(unverified)** were taken from secondary sources or from memory and need
checking before they are quoted anywhere public. For the general learning science (retrieval, spacing,
successive relearning), see [`web/docs/research/`](../../web/docs/research/).

**Bottom line.** No study shows that writing *retrieval answers* by hand beats typing them. The well-known
handwriting effect comes from lecture note-taking, and it is small and fragile. What is strong: retrieval
itself, drawing to remember, maps drawn from memory (as retrieval), and handwriting for mathematics and new
notation.

---

## 1. Handwriting vs typing

### Note-taking: contested, small at most

- **Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014)**, *Psychological Science* 25(6), 1159–1168. Laptop note-takers copied
  more verbatim, and longhand note-takers did better on conceptual questions.
- **Morehead, Dunlosky & Rawson (2019)**, *Educational Psychology Review* 31(3), 753–780. A direct
  replication, with an eWriter (digital ink) group added. The longhand edge on factual questions was small
  and not significant, there was no difference on conceptual questions, and the eWriter group did not
  differ either.
- **Urry et al. (2021)**, *Psychological Science* 32(3), 326–339. Laptops again produced more words and
  more verbatim notes, but the quiz advantage did not replicate. A mini meta-analysis of eight studies:
  small, non-significant effects favouring longhand.
- **Flanigan et al. (2024)**, *Educational Psychology Review* 36, art. 78. A meta-analysis of 24 studies:
  handwritten notes led to higher achievement (g = 0.25); typing recorded far more content (g = 0.92).
- **Voyer, Ronis & Byers (2022)**, *Contemporary Educational Psychology* 68. Reportedly little or no effect
  of medium once distraction is controlled. **(Pooled estimate unverified.)**
- **Bui, Myerson & Hale (2013)**, *Journal of Educational Psychology*. Verbatim transcription on a computer
  gave the best immediate recall, which undercuts the "verbatim is bad" mechanism.

### Single words and letters

- **Mangen et al. (2015)**, *Journal of Writing Research* 7(2). Free recall of handwritten words beat
  typed words (N = 36); recognition did not differ. Weak.
- **Richardson & Lacroix (2023/24)**, *Journal of Writing Research* 15(3). No handwriting advantage for free
  recall, but drawing beat both handwriting and typing.
- **Letter learning: moderate, and the strongest handwriting-specific evidence.** Longcamp et al. (2005),
  *Acta Psychologica*; James & Engelhardt (2012), *Trends in Neuroscience and Education*; **Wiley & Rapp
  (2021)**, *Psychological Science* 32(7): adults learning Arabic letters learned faster by writing than by
  typing or watching. This applies to new symbol systems such as mathematical notation, chemical
  structures and foreign scripts. It says nothing about learning biology facts.

### Brain imaging

- **Van der Weel & Van der Meer (2024)**, *Frontiers in Psychology* 14. Handwriting produced more
  widespread connectivity than typing. **It did not measure learning,** and a commentary (Pinet & Longcamp
  2025) criticises the typing condition: one finger, no visual feedback, uncontrolled skill. Do not cite it
  as evidence that handwriting improves learning.

### Answering retrieval questions specifically: no direct evidence

- **Putnam & Roediger (2013)**, *Memory & Cognition* 41: the testing effect was similar whether answers
  were spoken or written. **(Whether a handwriting condition was included is unverified.)**
- **Smith, Roediger & Karpicke (2013)**, *JEP:LMC*: covert (silent) retrieval helped about as much as overt
  retrieval.
- **Yu et al. (2025)**, *Educational Psychology Review* 37, art. 52: a meta-analysis of 18 studies. Overt
  retrieval beats covert, more so for complex material.
- **Takeaway:** producing an answer matters somewhat. Whether it is handwritten or typed has not been
  tested. DESIGN §11 proposes that the app test it.

## 2. Drawing

- **The drawing effect: strong.** Wammes, Meade & Fernandes (2016), *QJEP* 69(9): across seven experiments,
  drawing a word beat writing it, for free recall. Fernandes, Wammes & Meade (2018), *Current Directions*
  27(5): the effect extends to definitions and to older adults.
- **Learner-generated drawing for understanding: moderate, with conditions.** Fiorella & Zhang (2018),
  "Drawing boundary conditions for learning by drawing", *Educational Psychology Review* 30(3): the benefit
  needs guidance (partial drawings, comparison with an expert drawing) and costs time, and many studies use
  weak controls. Fiorella & Mayer (2016), "Eight ways to promote generative learning". This is why
  `sketch` items have structural rubrics and why map feedback shows the expert graph afterwards.
- Cromley, Du & Dane (2020), *Journal of Science Education and Technology* 29, compare drawing on a tablet
  with drawing on paper. **(Findings unverified.)**

## 3. Maps drawn from memory

- **Karpicke & Blunt (2011)**, *Science* 331: free-recall retrieval beat building a concept map with the
  text in view.
- **Blunt & Karpicke (2014)**, *Journal of Educational Psychology* 106(3): a concept map built *from
  memory* worked as well as written free recall.
- **O'Day & Karpicke (2021)**: mapping before retrieval added nothing.
- **Mayrhofer et al. (2023)**, *Frontiers in Psychology*, preregistered, N = 230: the retrieval advantage
  vanished when the mapping group got equal time and the same "recall as much as you can" instruction.
- **Takeaway:** a map drawn from memory is a valid retrieval format. The benefit comes from retrieving, not
  from the map's shape (DESIGN §6.6).

## 4. Self-explanation and mathematics

- **Bisra et al. (2018)**, *Educational Psychology Review* 30: self-explanation, g = 0.55. Strong.
- **Lachner, Jacob & Hoogerheide (2021)**, *Learning and Instruction* 74: explaining had a small effect
  (g = 0.22), and **explaining orally beat explaining in writing**. This is why voice is an open question
  for teach-back.
- **Maths input: moderate, and handwriting's clearest practical win.** Anthony, Yang & Koedinger (CHI 2005;
  later AIED and IJHCS papers): handwriting maths was about three times faster than an equation editor,
  and learners finished in about half the time with equal post-test scores. A 2022 study (*Informatics in
  Education* 21(1)) found handwriting faster, with more problems solved, but no learning difference.

## 5. Does slower writing force summarising?

Weak. The idea comes from note-taking, where a speaker sets the pace. In self-paced retrieval there is no
speaker, so slower writing does not obviously force you to generate rather than transcribe. Nobody has
tested it. The app does not claim it.

## 6. Digital ink vs paper

Weak, but it suggests a stylus keeps most of whatever benefit exists. Osugi et al. (2019) and Ihara et al.
(2021), *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, found no behavioural differences between pen and stylus.
Umejima et al. (2021) found paper better only on simple items, in a study funded by industry. Gerth et al.
(2016): tablet friction changes how beginners move when writing.

## 7. Costs

- **Throughput.** Handwritten prose is slower, which means fewer retrievals per minute, and retrieval count
  drives the testing effect. This is inferred, not tested, but it is why DESIGN §8 exists.
- **Accessibility.** Dysgraphia is commonly estimated at about 7–15% of school-age children **(secondary
  sources, wide range)**. Tremor and motor impairments add to that. A handwriting-only design needs a
  fallback (DESIGN §10).

## 8. Technology

### PencilKit and Apple Pencil

- `PKDrawing` → `PKStroke` → `PKStrokePath` → `PKStrokePoint`. Each point has a location, a time offset,
  size, opacity, force, azimuth and altitude (stored compressed, so not exact).
- **iPadOS 27 (WWDC26, "Read between the strokes with PencilKit"):** `PKStrokeRecognizer` does on-device
  handwriting recognition in 29 languages, with `recognizedText()`, candidate readings and `search(_:)`
  returning bounding boxes. It has **no maths support and no confidence scores**. Stroke IDs are now stable.
  **(Taken from the session as reported. Verify against the SDK.)**
- **Apple Pencil Pro:** squeeze (`onPencilSqueeze`), barrel roll (`UITouch.rollAngle`), haptics
  (`UICanvasFeedbackGenerator`). Hover via `UIHoverGestureRecognizer.zOffset` on M2 and later iPads.
- **Scribble** turns handwriting into text in text fields and throws the ink away. That is the wrong tool
  here.

### Recognising ink

- **Vision OCR** is built for printed text, and Apple publishes no handwriting accuracy figures. Running it
  on rendered ink throws away the strokes. Prefer `PKStrokeRecognizer` for text.
- **Math Notes / Smart Script:** no public developer API found.
- **MyScript iink SDK:** on-device recognition of text, maths and diagrams. Commercial per-device licence,
  with a periodic check-in to its licence server. **(Pricing unverified.)**
- **Google ML Kit Digital Ink:** runs on iOS, 300+ languages, shapes. No maths.
- **Mathpix:** image API at about $0.002 per image, and a **strokes endpoint (`v3/strokes`)** that takes raw
  stroke data, billed per session ($0.005–0.01 after 1,000 free). The best fit for handwritten maths sent
  as strokes. **(Pricing from an undated page.)**

### Grading handwriting with a multimodal model

- **Caraeni, Scarlatos & Lan** (LAK 2025, arXiv 2411.05231): GPT-4o grading handwritten probability exams
  was not accurate enough to use without a reference solution and a rubric. Errors came from misreading
  both the handwriting and the reasoning.
- **Kortemeyer et al.** (arXiv 2510.05162): GPT-5 on calculus work had moderate agreement with human
  graders. A confidence filter reached human-level accuracy but sent about 70% of items to humans.
- **arXiv 2605.19043:** models *hallucinated text resembling the reference solution*, inflating scores.
- **OmniHandwritingOCR** (arXiv 2608.18586): models fix the writer's mistakes, insert plausible missing
  steps and drop symbols they cannot read.
- **Henkel et al.** (arXiv 2510.05538): about 95% agreement on simple arithmetic, much worse on drawings.
- No published results specific to Claude were found.
- **Practice adopted in DESIGN §5:** transcribe without the reference, preferring stroke-based recognisers;
  have the learner confirm (fixes to misreadings only); grade the confirmed text blind; give drawings a
  separate, lower-confidence path.

### Apple Foundation Models

- iPadOS 26: an on-device text model with guided generation and tool calling.
- iPadOS 27 (WWDC26) **(as reported, verify)**: image input on device, an 8K context, a Private Cloud
  Compute model with a 32K context, and a `LanguageModel` protocol with Anthropic and Google Swift packages.
- Good for small private jobs: classifying, tagging, short wording. Not good as a reader or grader for
  handwritten maths or explanations.
